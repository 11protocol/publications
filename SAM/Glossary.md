# GovCon Terminology Glossary (Expanded)

- **8(a) Program**: A Small Business Administration certification that gives disadvantaged-owned small businesses access to set-aside and sole-source federal contracts for up to nine years.

- **Acquisition Lifecycle**: The full process from identifying a need to awarding and managing a contract.

- **Are we shaping it?**: Asking whether the company is influencing requirements before they are finalized.

- **Award Fee**: Extra money a contractor can earn based on strong performance.

- **BAA (Broad Agency Announcement)**: A notice inviting companies to propose solutions to broad problems, usually for research and development.

- **Base Year / Option Year**: The initial contract period followed by optional extensions the government may choose to exercise.

- **Basis of Estimate (BOE)**: The explanation and data behind how pricing or labor hours were calculated.

- **Bid Bond**: A financial guarantee that a bidder will honor its proposal if selected.

- **Bid Candy**: An attractive-looking opportunity that is unlikely to be worth pursuing.

- **Bid/No-Bid Decision**: Internal decision on whether to pursue an opportunity.

- **Black Hat Review**: Internal exercise where the team analyzes a deal from a competitor’s perspective.

- **Blanket Purchase Agreement (BPA)**: A pre-arranged agreement allowing repeated purchases without new competitions.

- **Bridge Contract**: A short-term contract used to continue services until a long-term contract is awarded.

- **CAGE Code (Commercial and Government Entity Code)**: A unique identifier assigned to companies doing business with the federal government.

- **Capability Statement**: A short document describing a company’s capabilities, experience, and qualifications.

- **Capture Management**: The process of positioning a company to win a contract before it is formally released.

- **Ceiling Value**: The maximum amount the government could spend under a contract.

- **Champion**: An internal government advocate who supports a specific company’s solution.

- **CLIN (Contract Line Item Number)**: A numbered item in a contract representing a specific deliverable or service.

- **Color of Money**: Rules governing how different types of government funds can be used.

- **Color Team Reviews**: Structured proposal reviews at different stages, such as Pink (early), Red (detailed), and Gold (final).

- **Compliance Matrix**: A checklist ensuring every requirement in a solicitation is addressed in the proposal.

- **Competitive Intelligence**: Information about competitors, incumbents, and likely strategies.

- **Competitive Range**: The group of bidders still being considered after initial evaluations.

- **Cost-Plus Contract**: A contract where the government reimburses costs and adds a fee.

- **COR (Contracting Officer’s Representative)**: Government official managing day-to-day contract performance.

- **CO (Contracting Officer)**: Government official with authority to sign and manage contracts.

- **CPARS (Contractor Performance Assessment Reporting System)**: Government database of contractor performance evaluations.

- **Customer Intimacy**: Depth of understanding and relationship with the government buyer.

- **Debrief**: A meeting where a losing bidder learns why they were not selected.

- **Deficiency**: A serious failure in a proposal that can lead to disqualification.

- **DFARS (Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement)**: Additional procurement rules for the Department of Defense.

- **Discriminator**: A clear feature or strength that differentiates a company from competitors.

- **Discussions**: Negotiation phase where proposals can be clarified or improved.

- **Downselect**: Process of narrowing the number of competing bidders.

- **Draft RFP (Draft Request for Proposal)**: Early version of a solicitation released for feedback.

- **End-of-Year Spend**: Surge in government spending before funds expire at fiscal year end.

- **Evaluation Criteria**: The factors used by the government to assess proposals.

- **Final Proposal Revision (FPR)**: The last version of a proposal submitted after discussions.

- **FAR (Federal Acquisition Regulation)**: The main rulebook governing federal procurement.

- **Firm-Fixed-Price (FFP) Contract**: A contract with a set price where the contractor bears cost risk.

- **FPDS (Federal Procurement Data System)**: Database tracking federal contract actions.

- **Full and Open Competition**: Procurement open to all qualified companies.

- **GAO Protest (Government Accountability Office Protest)**: Formal challenge to a contract award decision.

- **Gate Review**: Internal checkpoint to decide whether to continue pursuing a deal.

- **Ghosting**: Proposal technique highlighting competitor weaknesses without naming them.

- **Gold Team Review**: Final proposal review before submission.

- **GSA Schedule / MAS (Multiple Award Schedule)**: Pre-approved contract vehicle for streamlined purchasing.

- **GWAC (Government-Wide Acquisition Contract)**: Contract vehicle used across multiple federal agencies.

- **HUBZone (Historically Underutilized Business Zone)**: Certification for businesses in economically disadvantaged areas.

- **Incumbent**: The current contract holder.

- **Industry Day**: Event where agencies share upcoming opportunities with vendors.

- **IDIQ (Indefinite Delivery/Indefinite Quantity)**: Contract allowing flexible ordering over time.

- **Is it funded?**: Question about whether money has been officially allocated.

- **Is it real?**: Question about whether an opportunity will actually materialize.

- **Is it wired?**: Question about whether a winner has already been informally decided.

- **J&A (Justification and Approval)**: Document required to award contracts without full competition.

- **Kickoff Meeting**: Initial meeting after contract award to align expectations.

- **LPTA (Lowest Price Technically Acceptable)**: Award method based on lowest price meeting minimum requirements.

- **Market Research**: Government effort to understand available solutions before procurement.

- **Mentor-Protégé Program**: Program pairing large and small businesses for development and joint bidding.

- **Multiple Award Contract**: Contract awarded to several companies competing for task orders.

- **Must-Win**: Opportunity considered critical to business success.

- **NAICS Code (North American Industry Classification System Code)**: Industry classification used to determine business size and eligibility.

- **New Business**: Opportunities that are not tied to an existing contract.

- **Novation**: Transfer of a contract from one company to another.

- **OCI (Organizational Conflict of Interest)**: Situation where a company’s interests may bias contract work.

- **Ombudsman**: Neutral official handling procurement concerns.

- **Opportunity Lifecycle**: Stages from early identification to contract award.

- **Oral Presentation**: Live presentation as part of proposal evaluation.

- **Past Performance**: Record of previous contract performance.

- **Period of Performance (PoP)**: Timeframe during which contract work is performed.

- **Paper Exercise**: Competition where the outcome is likely already decided.

- **Pink Team Review**: Early proposal review focused on structure and messaging.

- **Pipeline**: All opportunities being tracked by a company.

- **Pipeline Coverage**: Ratio of total pipeline value to revenue target.

- **PWin (Probability of Win)**: Estimated likelihood of winning a deal.

- **PMO (Program Management Office)**: Team responsible for managing a program or contract.

- **Pre-Solicitation Notice**: Early signal that a formal solicitation is coming.

- **Prime Contractor**: Company directly holding the contract with the government.

- **Price-to-Win (PTW)**: Estimated price needed to win a contract.

- **Proposal Volumes**: Sections of a proposal such as technical, past performance, and price.

- **Protest**: Formal challenge to a procurement decision.

- **PSC (Product Service Code)**: Code identifying the type of product or service being purchased.

- **PWS (Performance Work Statement)**: Document describing required outcomes.

- **QASP (Quality Assurance Surveillance Plan)**: Plan for monitoring contractor performance.

- **Qualified Opportunity**: Opportunity that is real and worth pursuing.

- **Recompete**: Re-bidding of an existing contract.

- **Red Team Review**: Detailed proposal review simulating government evaluation.

- **RFI (Request for Information)**: Request for input from industry.

- **RFP (Request for Proposal)**: Formal request for detailed proposals.

- **RFQ (Request for Quotation)**: Request for pricing on defined requirements.

- **SAM.gov (System for Award Management)**: Official system for vendor registration and opportunity tracking.

- **SBIR / STTR (Small Business Innovation Research / Small Business Technology Transfer)**: Programs funding small business innovation.

- **SDVOSB (Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business)**: Certification for veteran-owned businesses.

- **Sources Sought Notice**: Request to identify capable vendors.

- **Strength**: Positive aspect of a proposal.

- **Subcontractor**: Company supporting the prime contractor.

- **Teaming Agreement**: Agreement between companies to pursue work together.

- **Technical Volume**: Section explaining how work will be performed.

- **Unfunded Requirement**: Need without approved budget.

- **Weakness**: Area of concern in a proposal.

- **Win Theme**: Core message explaining why a company should win.

- **We’re chasing shadows**: Pursuing opportunities that are unlikely to materialize.

- **This will slip**: Expectation that timeline will be delayed.
